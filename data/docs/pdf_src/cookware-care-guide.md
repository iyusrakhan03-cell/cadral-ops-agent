# Cookware Care Guide

Version 1.6 — effective 15 January 2026

Good cookware rewards a little care. This guide explains how to use, clean and store your Thistle & Pine pans, including our Pine Pro cast iron and Pine Pro stainless steel ranges and our everyday non-stick pans. Following it will keep your pans performing well and protect your warranty.

## 1. Pine Pro cast iron

Pine Pro cast iron skillets, casseroles and griddles come pre-seasoned, so they're ready to use straight away. Seasoning is a layer of oil baked onto the surface, which protects the iron from rust and builds a natural non-stick finish over time.

### Cooking with cast iron

- Heat cast iron gradually on a low to medium heat. It holds heat very well, so you rarely need a high flame.
- Never heat an empty pan on high for long periods. Overheating isn't covered by the Pine Pro guarantee.
- Cast iron is oven safe up to 260°C and works on gas, electric, ceramic and induction hobs, and in the oven.
- Handles get very hot. Always use an oven glove.
- Lift rather than slide pans on glass and ceramic hobs to avoid scratching the hob.

### Cleaning cast iron

- Hand wash only. Pine Pro cast iron is not dishwasher safe. Dishwasher damage to cast iron is not covered by the guarantee.
- Wash with hot water and a brush or sponge while the pan is still warm. A small amount of washing-up liquid is fine.
- For stuck-on food, scrub with coarse salt and a little water.
- Dry immediately and thoroughly with a towel, then place on a low heat for a minute to drive off any remaining moisture.
- While the pan is still warm, rub a thin layer of cooking oil over the inside surface with kitchen paper.
- Don't soak cast iron or leave it in water, as it will rust.

### If rust appears

Light surface rust isn't the end of your pan. Scrub the rust away with steel wool, wash, dry thoroughly and re-season: coat the whole pan in a thin layer of oil and bake it upside down in the oven for an hour, staying within the 260°C limit.

## 2. Pine Pro stainless steel

Our Pine Pro stainless steel frying pans, saucepans and stockpots have a tri-ply base for even heating.

### Cooking with stainless steel

- Pine Pro stainless steel is induction compatible and works on all hob types.
- Oven safe up to 220°C, including the lids.
- Preheat the pan for a minute or two before adding oil, then add food once the oil shimmers. This helps stop food sticking.
- Add salt to water only once it's boiling, to avoid small white pitting marks.

### Cleaning stainless steel

- Pine Pro stainless steel is dishwasher safe.
- For burnt-on food, fill the pan with water, bring it to the boil and use a wooden spatula to lift the residue.
- Rainbow-coloured heat marks are normal and can be removed with a little white vinegar.

## 3. Non-stick pans

Our everyday non-stick pans are ideal for eggs, pancakes and delicate fish.

### Cooking with non-stick

- Use a low to medium heat. High heat shortens the life of any non-stick coating.
- Avoid metal utensils. Use wooden, silicone or nylon utensils instead. Scratches caused by metal utensils are not covered by the warranty.
- Oven safe up to 180°C.
- Avoid aerosol cooking sprays, which can build up a sticky residue.

### Cleaning non-stick

- Non-stick pans are dishwasher safe, but hand washing with warm soapy water and a soft sponge will extend their life.
- Never use abrasive pads or scourers.
- Allow the pan to cool before washing. Plunging a hot pan into cold water can warp it.

## 4. Storing your cookware

- Make sure every pan is completely dry before putting it away.
- If you stack pans, place a pan protector or a folded tea towel between them to protect the surfaces.
- Store cast iron somewhere dry, with the lid off or ajar to let air circulate.
